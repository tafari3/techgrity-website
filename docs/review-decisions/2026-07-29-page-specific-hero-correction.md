# Page-Specific Hero Correction

**Date:** 29 July 2026  
**Templates:** Technology Supply & Integration; Industries Overview  
**Decision:** REVISION REQUIRED, THEN REBUILT FOR USER REVIEW

## Problem identified

The first hero concepts used generic technology photography and multiple overlapping images. They communicated broad technology coverage, but did not explain the specific purpose of either page.

This violated the design principle that every hero must communicate one page-specific message in the first viewport.

## Corrected Technology Supply & Integration hero

The revised hero removes the generic team photograph and uses one coherent delivery-control visual showing:

1. technical specification and bill of materials;
2. equipment staging and pre-delivery configuration;
3. commissioning, evidence and operational handover.

The hero now communicates that Techgrity does not merely deliver equipment. It controls the path from requirement through accepted operation.

## Corrected Industries Overview hero

The revised hero removes the team, data-centre and telecom collage. It uses one sector operating map that connects the six approved industries to shared technology layers:

- applications;
- data;
- connectivity;
- compute;
- power and security;
- support.

The hero now communicates that the solution changes by sector while the delivery discipline remains consistent.

## Locked hero-design rule

Before producing any future hero, the following questions must be answered in writing:

1. What single idea must the visitor understand within five seconds?
2. What visual form explains that idea most directly?
3. Does the hero require photography, a diagram, an interface, an operational scene or no image at all?
4. Is every visual element necessary to the page message?
5. Could the same hero be placed on another page without feeling wrong?

If the answer to question 5 is yes, the hero is too generic and must be redesigned.

## Review status

Both corrected desktop heroes are ready for user review. They are not approved until the user explicitly accepts them.
