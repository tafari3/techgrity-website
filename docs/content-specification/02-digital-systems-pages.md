## 1. Digital Systems & AI Landing Page

### Route

`/capabilities/digital-systems`

### Audience and user intent

Institutional leaders, technology managers and procurement evaluators want to understand whether Techgrity can design and deliver substantial software and digital operating systems rather than only websites.

### Business objective

Establish a serious Digital Systems division and route visitors to the five distinct digital capability pages.

### SEO intent

Custom software, artificial intelligence, workflow automation, enterprise applications, systems integration, data platforms and digital access in Zimbabwe and Africa.

### Browser title

`Digital Systems & AI | Techgrity Systems`

### H1

> Digital systems built around how organisations actually operate

### Hero support

> Techgrity designs and integrates software, artificial intelligence, workflow automation, data platforms and secure digital services that connect people, information and operational responsibilities.

Primary CTA: `Discuss a Digital Project`  
Secondary CTA: `Explore Digital Capabilities`

### Ordered section structure

#### 1. Digital Systems positioning

Explain that Techgrity begins with the organisational requirement and operating model, then designs applications, workflows, integrations, data and controls as one system.

#### 2. Organisational problems addressed

Examples:

- manual and fragmented processes;
- disconnected applications;
- duplicated or unreliable data;
- limited operational visibility;
- unclear roles and approvals;
- weak evidence and audit trails;
- systems that do not reflect actual work;
- poor integration between digital and physical infrastructure.

The copy must not suggest every client has all problems.

#### 3. Five digital capability routes

1. Software, AI & Enterprise Applications
2. Workflow & Process Automation
3. Systems Integration
4. Data Platforms & Analytics
5. Cybersecurity & Digital Access

Each card must include scope, outcome and canonical link.

#### 4. Types of systems

Representative categories:

- institutional platforms;
- operational management systems;
- web and mobile applications;
- citizen, customer, student or staff portals;
- workflow and approval platforms;
- dashboards and reporting systems;
- integration services;
- intelligent assistants and decision-support tools.

These are capability examples, not public product claims.

#### 5. Architecture and integration

H2:

> Designed as part of the wider operating environment

Explain:

- existing systems and data sources;
- identities, roles and permissions;
- infrastructure and connectivity;
- external services and APIs;
- reliability and support requirements;
- future extension and maintainability.

#### 6. Artificial intelligence with human accountability

Explain appropriate AI use:

- information retrieval and assistance;
- classification and routing;
- summarisation and analysis;
- decision support;
- workflow acceleration;
- human review and approval;
- logging and governance.

Avoid autonomous or accuracy claims that cannot be guaranteed.

#### 7. Security, controls and evidence

Cover role-based access, approvals, audit events, data handling, monitoring, change control and security-conscious design.

#### 8. Delivery lifecycle

Show Discover, Design, Build, Integrate, Validate, Commission and Support. Link to `/how-we-deliver` for the complete corporate lifecycle.

#### 9. Support and maintenance

Explain possible support areas without promising unapproved SLAs:

- defect correction;
- controlled changes;
- platform maintenance;
- monitoring where agreed;
- user and administrator support;
- documentation and knowledge transfer.

#### 10. Industries

Link to all relevant industry pages, with emphasis on government, education, telecommunications, energy, technology organisations and commerce/logistics.

#### 11. Related infrastructure

Explain that digital systems may depend on networks, data centres, cloud environments, power resilience, security and technical facilities.

#### 12. Final CTA

H2:

> Replace fragmented tools with an operational system designed around the organisation

Primary CTA: `Discuss a Digital Project`

### Imagery and diagrams

- people using operational systems in a credible institutional environment;
- workflow and solution-architecture diagrams;
- interface previews only when they are clearly conceptual or based on approved real work;
- no humanoid robots or glowing AI brains.

### Factual dependencies

- approved wording for AI governance;
- any support model or response time;
- any named technology partners or platforms.

### Acceptance criteria

- page cannot be mistaken for a web-design service page;
- all five capabilities are distinct;
- AI is presented with practical use and human accountability;
- security, integration and operational support are explicit;
- no unfinished product is exposed.

## 4. Systems Integration

### Route

`/capabilities/digital-systems/integration`

### Audience and user intent

A technical evaluator or operations leader has multiple systems, data sources or external services that must exchange information reliably and securely.

### Business objective

Position integration as a designed and monitored capability that reduces fragmentation and supports end-to-end operations.

### SEO intent

API integration, systems integration, legacy integration, identity integration and data exchange.

### Browser title

`Systems Integration | Techgrity Systems`

### H1

> Connect applications, data and operational services securely

### Hero support

> Techgrity designs and implements integrations that allow systems to exchange information, trigger actions and preserve clear responsibility across organisational boundaries.

Primary CTA: `Discuss an Integration`

### Ordered section structure

#### 1. Integration scope

- APIs;
- system-to-system integration;
- legacy application integration;
- data exchange;
- identity and access integration;
- payment, messaging or external service integration where appropriate;
- event-driven integration;
- files and scheduled exchange where modern APIs are unavailable;
- monitoring and failure handling.

#### 2. Common integration challenges

- duplicate entry;
- mismatched identifiers;
- inconsistent data definitions;
- brittle point-to-point connections;
- unclear ownership;
- silent failures;
- credentials managed insecurely;
- external dependency changes.

#### 3. Integration discovery

Cover systems, owners, interfaces, data, timing, security, volume, failure impact and support responsibilities.

#### 4. Integration architecture

Potential patterns:

- synchronous APIs;
- asynchronous messaging;
- event-driven workflows;
- scheduled data exchange;
- integration services or middleware;
- identity federation;
- controlled manual fallback.

No one pattern should be presented as universally correct.

#### 5. Data contracts and ownership

Explain schemas, identifiers, validation, source of truth, privacy, retention and responsibility for correcting failures.

#### 6. Security

Cover authentication between systems, least privilege, secret management, transport security, input validation, audit events and third-party risk.

#### 7. Reliability and observability

Cover retries, duplicate protection, idempotency where needed, queues, error classification, alerting, reconciliation and support evidence.

#### 8. Typical deliverables

- system and interface inventory;
- integration architecture;
- data mapping;
- API or connector implementation;
- test environment and test evidence;
- monitoring and error handling;
- operating documentation;
- handover.

#### 9. Delivery and commissioning

Explain integration testing, end-to-end user journeys, failure-path validation, cutover and post-commissioning observation.

#### 10. Related capabilities

- Software, AI & Enterprise Applications
- Workflow & Process Automation
- Data Platforms & Analytics
- Cybersecurity & Digital Access
- Data Centres & Cloud

#### 11. Final CTA

H2:

> Map the complete information journey before adding another connection

Primary CTA: `Discuss an Integration`

### Imagery and diagrams

- system-context diagram;
- data and event-flow visual;
- integration monitoring concept;

## 6. Cybersecurity & Digital Access

### Route

`/capabilities/digital-systems/cybersecurity-access`

### Audience and user intent

A technology, security or operational leader needs secure identities, permissions, application access, auditability and security-conscious implementation.

### Business objective

Present the security capabilities Techgrity can responsibly offer without implying unverified certifications, penetration-testing authority or a full managed security operations centre.

### SEO intent

Identity and access management, application security, role-based access, audit trails and secure systems implementation.

### Browser title

`Cybersecurity & Digital Access | Techgrity Systems`

### H1

> Control who can access systems, data and sensitive actions

### Hero support

> Techgrity designs identity, permissions, auditability and security controls into digital systems and their operating environments.

Primary CTA: `Discuss a Security Requirement`

### Ordered section structure

#### 1. Responsible capability boundary

State clearly that the launch service scope focuses on:

- identity and access management;
- authentication and role design;
- application and integration security;
- access reviews;
- audit trails;
- secure configuration and deployment;
- monitoring and security-conscious implementation;
- digital and physical access integration where relevant.

Do not claim specialised offensive security, certification, forensics or 24/7 managed detection unless formally established.

#### 2. Identity and authentication

Cover user lifecycle, organisational identity, authentication methods, federation where appropriate and recovery controls.

#### 3. Roles and permissions

Cover least privilege, role-based access, approval authority, sensitive actions and separation of duties.

#### 4. Auditability and evidence

Cover significant events, actor, time, action, outcome, immutable or protected records where needed, review and retention.

#### 5. Application and integration security

Cover input validation, session protection, secure interfaces, secrets, dependency management and environment separation at an appropriate high level.

#### 6. Access reviews and operational controls

Cover joiner, mover and leaver processes; periodic reviews; privileged access; emergency access; controlled changes.

#### 7. Monitoring and response support

Use bounded wording around logging, alerting, incident escalation and support. Do not imply a security operations centre.

#### 8. Digital and physical access integration

Explain potential integration with access-control systems, visitor management and facilities while linking to Security & Smart Facilities.

#### 9. Typical deliverables

- access and role model;
- authentication design;
- permission matrix;
- audit-event design;
- security requirements;
- secure configuration guidance;
- access-review process;
- testing and acceptance evidence;
- operating documentation.

#### 10. Related capabilities

- Software, AI & Enterprise Applications
- Workflow & Process Automation
- Systems Integration
- Data Platforms & Analytics
- Security & Smart Facilities

#### 11. Final CTA

H2:

> Make access and accountability part of the system design

Primary CTA: `Discuss a Security Requirement`

### Imagery and diagrams

- identity, role and resource relationship diagram;
- secure access journey;
- human-centred authentication imagery;
- avoid hooded-attacker stock images and excessive padlock graphics.

### Factual dependencies

- exact service boundary;
- approved technology platforms;
- any certification, monitoring or incident-response statement;
- physical access integration partners.

### Acceptance criteria

- service boundary is explicit;
- no unsupported managed-security or offensive-security claim appears;
- identity, permissions, audit and secure implementation are well explained;
- connection to physical access is clear but not overstated.
