## 4. How We Deliver

### Route

`/how-we-deliver`

### Audience and user intent

A decision-maker, technical evaluator or procurement reviewer wants to know how Techgrity controls scope, delivery, quality, integration, commissioning and handover.

### Business objective

Establish delivery credibility and reduce perceived execution risk.

### SEO intent

Technology project delivery, integration, commissioning, support and implementation methodology.

### Browser title

`How We Deliver | Techgrity Systems`

### H1

> From requirement to operational handover

### Hero support

> Techgrity structures delivery around defined responsibilities, technical evidence, controlled decisions and measurable operational outcomes.

Primary CTA: `Discuss a Project`  
Secondary CTA: `Explore Capabilities`

### Ordered section structure

#### 1. Delivery model introduction

Explain that the lifecycle applies across software, infrastructure and combined projects while allowing the controls and evidence to be adapted to the project's scale and risk.

#### 2. Seven-stage lifecycle

**1. Discover**

- stakeholder and user needs;
- current environment;
- constraints, dependencies and risks;
- required outcomes;
- initial scope boundary.

**2. Design**

- solution architecture;
- specifications;
- integration design;
- implementation and acceptance plan;
- security and operational considerations.

**3. Source**

- bills of materials;
- supplier and partner coordination;
- licensing and compatibility checks;
- delivery scheduling;
- change control.

**4. Build**

- software development or infrastructure implementation;
- configuration;
- controlled environments;
- quality checks;
- progress evidence.

**5. Integrate**

- system interfaces;
- identity and access;
- data exchange;
- network and platform integration;
- operational workflow alignment.

**6. Commission**

- testing against acceptance criteria;
- defect correction;
- documentation;
- training;
- controlled operational handover.

**7. Support**

- monitoring where agreed;
- maintenance;
- incident and change handling;
- knowledge transfer;
- continuous improvement under defined support terms.

#### 3. Responsibilities and governance

H2:

> Clear responsibilities before work begins

Cover:

- accountable owner;
- decision and approval routes;
- scope and change control;
- risk ownership;
- partner responsibilities;
- evidence and reporting.

#### 4. Quality, security and risk

Explain that these are built into design and delivery rather than added at the end.

#### 5. Testing and acceptance

Cover:

- agreed acceptance criteria;
- functional and technical testing;
- integration testing;
- performance or resilience testing where relevant;
- defect classification;
- retesting;
- acceptance evidence.

#### 6. Documentation and handover

Typical deliverables may include:

- architecture and design documents;
- configuration records;
- asset or equipment schedules;
- test and commissioning results;
- operating procedures;
- training records;
- support and escalation information.

Wording must remain conditional because deliverables depend on project scope.

#### 7. Partner-led specialist delivery

Explain that Techgrity may coordinate qualified specialists, suppliers and OEM-aligned delivery where required, while preserving clear responsibilities and one governed project structure. Do not imply formal vendor status unless verified.

#### 8. Final CTA

H2:

> Start with the requirement, not a predetermined product

Copy:

> Tell us what the organisation must achieve, the environment in which the solution will operate and the constraints that matter.

Primary CTA: `Discuss a Project`

### Imagery and diagrams

- authoritative seven-stage lifecycle graphic;
- responsibility or evidence-flow diagram;
- selective delivery photography, not generic project-management stock imagery.

### Factual dependencies

- approved wording for partner-delivery model;
- any response-time or support claim must be based on an actual service commitment.

### Acceptance criteria

- all seven stages are distinct and logically connected;
- the page communicates controls and evidence, not generic promises;
- no unsupported SLA, certification or partner claim appears;
- commissioning and operational handover are explicit.

## 8. Discuss a Project

### Route

`/discuss-a-project`

### Audience and user intent

A qualified prospect, institution, procurement team or partner wants to describe a real requirement and start a structured business conversation.

### Business objective

Capture enough information to qualify and route the opportunity without making the form unnecessarily difficult to complete.

### SEO intent

Project enquiry for software, infrastructure, networks, data centres, telecoms, power and integrated technology delivery.

### Browser title

`Discuss a Project | Techgrity Systems`

### H1

> Tell us about the outcome your organisation needs

### Hero support

> Share the requirement, operating environment, constraints and expected timescale. Techgrity will use the information to identify the appropriate capability and next step.

### Ordered section structure

#### 1. Before you begin

Briefly explain:

- incomplete early-stage requirements are acceptable;
- the organisation should not submit passwords, private keys or unnecessary sensitive personal information;
- attachments should be limited to relevant non-secret project or tender documents;
- submission does not constitute a contract or guaranteed acceptance.

#### 2. Project enquiry form

Group fields into logical steps.

**Step 1 — Contact**

- name;
- organisation;
- role;
- work email;
- telephone;
- country.

**Step 2 — Requirement**

- project category;
- industry;
- project location;
- project description;
- expected outcomes;
- current environment.

**Step 3 — Delivery context**

- required timescale;
- procurement or tender status;
- approximate budget range, optional;
- required documents, optional;
- attachment where securely supported.

**Step 4 — Consent and submission**

- privacy acknowledgement;
- consent checkbox;
- clear submit label: `Submit Project Enquiry`.

#### 3. What happens next

Use process wording without unsupported response-time promises:

1. enquiry is reviewed and routed;
2. Techgrity may request clarification;
3. an appropriate discovery or capability discussion may be arranged;
4. any scope, proposal or commercial commitment is handled separately.

#### 4. Alternative contact

Provide business email and telephone for visitors unable to use the form.

### Project categories

- Digital Systems & AI
- Networks & Fibre
- Data Centres & Cloud
- Telecom & Radio
- Power & Energy
- Security & Smart Facilities
- Civil & Technical Infrastructure
- Technology Supply & Integration
- Multiple or integrated capabilities

### Imagery and diagrams

The page is task-oriented. Use minimal supporting imagery and a calm progress indicator. Do not place a distracting full-width stock image beside the form.

### Factual dependencies

- recipient mailbox and routing rules;
- attachment policy;
- privacy policy and consent wording;
- budget-range decision and currency presentation;
- operational follow-up process.

### Acceptance criteria

- form is fully usable by keyboard and mobile;
- field labels, help text and errors are clear;
- successful and failed submissions are explicitly handled;
- no entered content is silently lost on validation failure;
- spam protection does not create an inaccessible barrier;
- attachment controls are secure or omitted;
- no response-time or commercial promise is made without approval.
