# Techgrity Systems Image and Art-Direction Brief

**Status:** LOCKED FOR MASTER-VISUAL PRODUCTION  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 29 July 2026  
**Depends on:** [`03-visual-design-system.md`](03-visual-design-system.md)

## 1. Purpose

This document defines what every image must communicate, how it must be composed, how it must behave responsively and what must be rejected before it enters the Techgrity Systems website.

The image system must support the corporate proposition:

> **Digital systems. Critical infrastructure. One accountable partner.**

Images are evidence-bearing communication components, not decoration. Every image must have a defined purpose and must remain technically plausible.

## 2. Governing visual direction

The controlling image style is:

> **Premium documentary realism with warm corporate grading and engineering precision.**

The complete site should feel like one organisation operating across software, institutional technology and critical infrastructure. Digital and infrastructure images may differ in subject and density, but must share:

- realistic light;
- credible environments;
- controlled navy shadows;
- restrained warm highlights;
- natural skin tones;
- clean technical geometry;
- purposeful composition;
- no exaggerated visual effects.

## 3. Absolute exclusions

Reject imagery containing:

- futuristic robots, androids or humanoid AI;
- glowing holograms, neon circuits or science-fiction interfaces;
- staged handshakes as the main subject;
- generic teams smiling directly at camera without an operational context;
- fake server rooms or impossible cable arrangements;
- visible unlicensed third-party logos;
- unsafe work practices;
- implausible tower, electrical or fibre installations;
- repeated construction-site imagery across unrelated pages;
- generic city skylines that communicate nothing about the page;
- equipment catalogue cut-outs on white backgrounds unless used in a controlled technical specification graphic;
- embedded text that should remain live HTML;
- invented client signage, project plaques or certifications.

## 4. Representation and local credibility

Photography should reflect Zimbabwean and African operating environments without relying on stereotypes.

Requirements:

- represent African professionals as technical decision-makers, engineers, operators, researchers and users;
- use diverse age and gender representation where appropriate;
- show real work, collaboration, assessment, configuration, monitoring or commissioning;
- avoid tokenistic placement of people solely to signal geography;
- do not claim a specific Techgrity project location unless the image is verified and approved as project evidence;
- representative imagery must be labelled and treated as representative, not as documentary proof of completed work.

## 5. Technical quality requirements

### 5.1 Source quality

- master photographic source: minimum 3200 px on the long edge for major heroes;
- card photography: minimum 1600 px on the long edge;
- no visible compression artefacts at target display size;
- preserve sufficient negative space for responsive crops;
- do not upscale low-quality images to meet dimensions.

### 5.2 Delivery formats

Preferred delivery:

- AVIF for modern browsers where supported;
- WebP as broad modern fallback;
- JPEG for photographic fallback where necessary;
- PNG only for transparency, logos or graphics requiring lossless edges;
- SVG for line diagrams, icons and non-photographic technical graphics.

### 5.3 Aspect-ratio families

| Asset family | Primary ratio | Required alternate |
|---|---|---|
| homepage integrated hero | approximately 16:5 desktop | 4:5 or 9:16 mobile composition |
| internal split hero | 4:3 or 3:2 | 4:5 mobile |
| internal full-width hero | 16:7 | 4:5 mobile crop |
| capability card | 16:9 | 4:3 where grid requires |
| industry card | 4:3 | 1:1 compact crop |
| editorial support image | 3:2 | 4:3 |
| document thumbnail | A4 portrait representation | responsive fixed-ratio preview |
| social sharing image | 1200 × 630 | none; safe-area aware |

Every major image requires a desktop and mobile crop decision before implementation.

## 6. Colour and grading

### Digital Systems images

- neutral or softly warm whites;
- controlled teal details where naturally present;
- clean screens and workspaces;
- moderate contrast;
- natural, human-centred light;
- do not colour-grade every screen teal.

### Infrastructure images

- deeper navy shadows;
- stronger material texture;
- controlled warm sunlight or neutral technical lighting;
- clear equipment detail;
- slightly higher contrast than Digital Systems imagery;
- avoid dramatic disaster-film grading.

### Cross-division images

- combine human activity with visible systems or infrastructure;
- use visual transitions rather than hard collage borders where possible;
- maintain a single tonal grade across the composition.

## 7. People and screen rules

- people must be engaged in a credible task;
- faces may be visible, but the task and environment should remain central;
- screens must not expose personal data, credentials, confidential dashboards or third-party branding;
- synthetic interface content must be plausible and non-deceptive;
- do not imply a displayed interface is an existing Techgrity product unless it is approved implementation evidence;
- use shallow depth of field only when it does not obscure the technical subject.

## 8. Equipment and engineering accuracy

Before approval, infrastructure images must be checked for:

- credible cable routing and termination;
- appropriate rack and equipment orientation;
- safe PPE where the environment requires it;
- plausible antenna and tower geometry;
- realistic solar, battery and UPS configurations;
- credible CCTV and access-control placement;
- no impossible power or network connections;
- no obvious third-party trademark that implies a partnership.

Where technical certainty is unavailable, use a broader environmental composition rather than a close-up that may contain errors.

## 9. Diagram art direction

Technical diagrams must use the visual system rather than imitate engineering CAD drawings or futuristic interfaces.

Required style:

- navy structural lines;
- teal active paths and integration points;
- white, ivory or cool-light canvas;
- simple nodes and modules;
- concise live-text labels;
- arrows with clear direction;
- no glowing effects;
- no decorative pseudo-code;
- no meaningless data particles;
- accessible text equivalent for every complex visual.

Core diagram families:

1. integrated operating layers;
2. Discover-to-Support lifecycle;
3. software architecture and integration;
4. data collection, governance and analytics flow;
5. network and infrastructure topology abstraction;
6. telecom site and backhaul relationship;
7. resilient power layers;
8. physical and digital security integration;
9. requirement-to-handover supply process;
10. sector solution maps.

## 10. Homepage image specification

### Communication objective

Show that Techgrity connects people, digital systems, data-centre capability, telecommunications and power infrastructure as one accountable delivery partner.

### Desktop composition

- left: African technical team working around a live operational screen or workstation;
- centre: dark, controlled data-centre or digital-system depth;
- right: telecom tower in a credible urban or regional environment with restrained solar or power detail;
- hero copy remains central and legible;
- no hard-edged scrapbook collage;
- tower remains clearly visible on the right;
- people remain visible on the left;
- dark overlay unifies the image and protects text contrast.

### Mobile composition

- prioritise people plus one recognisable infrastructure signal;
- tower may remain in the upper or lower background but must not disappear entirely;
- remove unnecessary centre detail rather than shrinking the desktop collage;
- keep headline and CTAs clear of faces and critical equipment.

### Supporting homepage media

Capability cards require:

1. Software & AI — credible code, architecture or operational interface context;
2. Networks & Fibre — fibre or structured network detail with clean cable management;
3. Data Centres & Cloud — racks, containment or server environment;
4. Telecom Infrastructure — tower, antenna or microwave infrastructure;
5. Power & Energy — solar, UPS, batteries or resilient power context;
6. Technical Infrastructure — controlled technical facility, cable pathways, shelter or enabling works.

## 11. Master-template visual briefs

### 11.1 Capabilities overview

**Hero:** integrated institutional environment showing both digital and physical systems without repeating the homepage collage.  
**Primary diagram:** three-part operating model with Digital Systems, Infrastructure and Supply/Delivery connected around organisational outcomes.  
**Supporting media:** selective technical close-ups, not one image per bullet.  
**Avoid:** a dense icon grid.

### 11.2 Digital Systems landing

**Hero:** professional team reviewing a workflow, architecture or operational platform in a real institutional setting.  
**Primary diagram:** users, applications, data, integration, security and infrastructure layers.  
**Supporting media:** interface details, collaboration, data and system architecture.  
**Avoid:** robot imagery, floating holograms and generic laptop stock photos.

### 11.3 Digital capability detail

**Hero:** capability-specific operational scene with one clear system context.  
**Primary diagram:** scope-specific architecture or workflow.  
**Supporting media:** one people/process image and one technical visual where useful.  
**Avoid:** repeating the same developer-at-laptop photograph across all five pages.

### 11.4 Infrastructure landing

**Hero:** strong, broad infrastructure environment showing network, data-centre, telecom or power authority.  
**Primary diagram:** connected infrastructure layers from connectivity and compute through power, security and facilities.  
**Supporting media:** high-quality capability photography with consistent grading.  
**Avoid:** turning the page into a construction gallery.

### 11.5 Infrastructure capability detail

**Hero:** one technically credible capability environment.  
**Primary diagram:** assessment, design, installation, integration, testing and handover.  
**Supporting media:** close technical detail plus operating context.  
**Avoid:** product-catalogue pack shots and unsafe installation scenes.

### 11.6 Technology Supply & Integration

**Hero:** specification review, equipment staging or controlled commissioning environment.  
**Primary diagram:** requirement → specification → source → deliver → configure → integrate → commission → handover.  
**Supporting media:** bills of materials, labelled staging, testing and documentation.  
**Avoid:** shopping carts, shelves of consumer electronics and price-led product imagery.

### 11.7 Industries overview

**Hero:** multi-sector operating context expressed through people and systems, not a six-image collage.  
**Primary diagram:** people, applications/data, connectivity/compute, power/security/facilities and governance/support.  
**Supporting media:** six sector cards with distinct but harmonised imagery.

### 11.8 Industry detail

**Hero:** credible sector environment with room for page copy.  
**Primary diagram:** sector-specific integrated solution map.  
**Supporting media:** one operational image and selected capability images.  
**Avoid:** images that imply a specific client or completed project.

### 11.9 How We Deliver

**Hero:** multidisciplinary planning or commissioning scene showing responsibility and evidence, not a generic meeting.  
**Primary diagram:** seven-stage lifecycle.  
**Supporting media:** assessment, design, field implementation, testing, documentation and handover.  
**Avoid:** stock project-management charts and staged handshake imagery.

### 11.10 Company

**Hero:** confident, human corporate environment with technical context and Zimbabwean grounding.  
**Supporting media:** real team photography only when approved; otherwise use representative operational imagery and restrained diagrams.  
**Avoid:** fake office scale, fake leadership portraits and unverified location imagery.

### 11.11 Resources

**Hero:** clean editorial/document environment with subtle technical context.  
**Primary media:** real capability-statement covers and document previews once documents exist.  
**Avoid:** fake downloadable documents and “coming soon” cards.

### 11.12 Contact and enquiry forms

**Hero:** concise human and operational context with significant negative space.  
**Supporting media:** map only after exact public address is approved; otherwise use a restrained Harare or technical-office context that does not imply an unverified address.  
**Avoid:** call-centre stock photos and decorative maps.

## 13. Asset naming convention

Use predictable names:

```text
{page-or-template}-{role}-{subject}-{variant}.{ext}
```

Examples:

```text
home-hero-integrated-desktop.avif
home-hero-integrated-mobile.avif
networks-fibre-hero-fibre-operations-desktop.webp
networks-fibre-hero-fibre-operations-mobile.webp
how-we-deliver-diagram-lifecycle.svg
education-research-card-campus-network.webp
```

Do not use meaningless names such as `image1-final-new.jpg`.

## 14. Asset register fields

Every production image must be recorded with:

- asset filename;
- page and component;
- source or generation provenance;
- licence or ownership status;
- subject description;
- communication purpose;
- desktop crop;
- mobile crop;
- intrinsic dimensions;
- delivered formats;
- alternative text;
- whether decorative;
- approval status;
- approver and approval date;
- replacement or expiry notes where applicable.

## 15. Alternative-text rules

- describe the information the image contributes, not every visible object;
- do not begin with “image of” or “picture of”;
- do not repeat adjacent text;
- identify representative imagery only where that distinction matters;
- decorative background images use empty alternative text or CSS backgrounds;
- diagrams require a concise alt description plus a nearby complete text equivalent;
- do not claim people shown are Techgrity employees unless verified.

Example:

> Engineers reviewing an operational system beside network monitoring displays.

Not:

> Techgrity engineers successfully delivering a client project.

The second example is prohibited without verified project evidence and publication approval.

## 16. Generated-image rules

Generated visuals may be used for approved representative compositions when suitable licensed photography is unavailable, provided that:

- the output is clearly technically reviewed;
- no real client, project or partnership is implied;
- no protected brand or trademark is reproduced;
- people are presented respectfully;
- equipment geometry and safety are plausible;
- the asset register records generated provenance;
- the image is not treated as documentary project evidence.

Generated images must not replace real evidence when the website later claims a completed project.

## 17. Image approval checklist

An image is approved only when all answers are yes:

1. Does it communicate a defined page message?
2. Is the subject technically plausible?
3. Does it fit the warm-corporate-with-engineering-authority direction?
4. Is the image free of unsupported client, partner or certification implications?
5. Is the source, licence or generated provenance recorded?
6. Are desktop and mobile crops defined?
7. Is text contrast protected where text overlays the image?
8. Is alternative-text treatment defined?
9. Does it avoid the absolute exclusions?
10. Does it remain visually consistent with the approved homepage?
