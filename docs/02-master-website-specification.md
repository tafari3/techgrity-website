# Techgrity Systems Master Website Specification

**Status:** BASELINE DRAFT FOR CONTENT AND VISUAL DESIGN  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 29 July 2026  
**Depends on:** [`01-website-architecture.md`](01-website-architecture.md)

## 1. Purpose

This document defines what the Techgrity Systems corporate website must achieve, how it must behave and how completion will be proven. It governs content design, visual design, implementation, integrations, quality assurance and deployment.

The website is not merely a brochure. It must function as:

- Techgrity's primary corporate credibility surface;
- a clear explanation of its digital and infrastructure capabilities;
- a tender and procurement evaluation aid;
- a qualified project-enquiry channel;
- a controlled source of corporate capability documents;
- a scalable foundation for future verified projects, partners, insights and certifications.

## 2. Business objective

The website must position Techgrity Systems as one accountable Zimbabwean partner capable of delivering both digital systems and critical infrastructure through a structured, evidence-conscious delivery model.

It must increase the likelihood that a relevant visitor will:

1. understand what Techgrity does within one visit;
2. identify the correct capability or industry route;
3. trust Techgrity enough to continue evaluating the company;
4. download or request an appropriate capability statement;
5. submit a qualified project enquiry or contact business development.

## 3. Primary audiences

### 3.1 Institutional decision-makers

Examples:

- executives;
- government and public-sector officials;
- university and research leadership;
- operations and programme directors;
- technology and infrastructure leaders.

They need a concise corporate proposition, credible breadth, clear accountability and confidence that Techgrity understands operational outcomes rather than isolated products.

### 3.2 Technical evaluators

Examples:

- ICT managers;
- infrastructure engineers;
- network and data-centre teams;
- software architects;
- security and operations personnel.

They need specific capability scope, integration logic, delivery stages, technical discipline, documentation, commissioning and support expectations.

### 3.3 Procurement and tender evaluators

They need:

- unambiguous service categories;
- verifiable corporate information;
- delivery methodology;
- capability statements;
- public contact details;
- legal and policy pages;
- no unsupported claims.

### 3.4 Partners and specialist delivery organisations

They need to understand where Techgrity leads, where it integrates partners and how project responsibilities are governed.

### 3.5 Growing organisations

They need an understandable route into scalable software, connectivity, cloud, security, power and support without enterprise jargon becoming a barrier.

## 4. Brand proposition

### Primary headline

> Digital systems. Critical infrastructure. One accountable partner.

### Corporate supporting statement

> Techgrity Systems designs, builds, integrates and supports software platforms, networks, data centres, telecommunications, power systems and technical infrastructure for organisations across Zimbabwe and Africa.

### Brand character

The website must feel:

- competent;
- accountable;
- warm but authoritative;
- technically credible;
- locally grounded;
- structured;
- calm rather than flashy.

It must not feel like:

- a small web-design agency;
- an online electronics shop;
- a generic construction contractor;
- a speculative AI start-up;
- a stock-template corporate site;
- an organisation claiming unproven scale.

## 5. Design direction

The controlling visual direction is **warm corporate with engineering authority**.

### Keep

- warm ivory and pale neutral backgrounds;
- Techgrity navy and teal;
- spacious layouts;
- rounded but restrained components;
- human-centred photography;
- clear language;
- friendly calls to action.

### Add

- high-quality fibre, data-centre, telecom and energy photography;
- restrained technical line diagrams;
- network and project-lifecycle graphics;
- subtle grid or blueprint motifs;
- stronger dark-navy sections;
- structured capability matrices;
- precise labels and specifications.

### Avoid

- construction-site imagery on every page;
- repetitive smiling-office stock photography;
- futuristic robots;
- excessive gradients;
- glowing technology effects;
- dozens of decorative icons;
- catalogue-style product grids;
- visual clutter.

## 6. Divisional visual distinction

### 6.1 Digital Systems pages

- lighter backgrounds;
- workflow and people-oriented photography;
- interface previews and architecture diagrams;
- teal emphasis;
- softer section transitions;
- content focused on systems, information and operational processes.

### 6.2 Infrastructure pages

- stronger navy presence;
- technical photography;
- structured capability sections;
- engineering diagrams and project phases;
- more architectural spacing;
- content focused on physical systems, specifications, integration and commissioning.

Both divisions must retain the same logo, typography, navigation, component system and corporate voice.

## 24. Content rules

All content must be:

- factual;
- specific;
- understandable to both decision-makers and technical evaluators;
- written in clear international English;
- free of unsupported superlatives;
- free of invented project evidence;
- consistent in terminology;
- written around organisational outcomes and accountable delivery.

Use “we can”, “we design”, “we provide” or “typical project types” where evidence of completed delivery is not yet public. Do not convert planned capability into fabricated past performance.

## 25. Image and media rules

Every image must have a defined communication purpose.

Required qualities:

- technically plausible content;
- strong composition;
- appropriate subject diversity;
- no visible third-party trademarks unless licensed and intentional;
- consistent grading and resolution;
- desktop and mobile crop plans;
- meaningful alternative text when informative;
- empty alternative text when purely decorative.

Avoid embedding important text inside images. Critical information must remain live HTML.

## 26. Accessibility requirements

Target: **WCAG 2.2 Level AA** for the implemented public website.

Minimum requirements:

- complete keyboard navigation;
- visible focus states;
- correct heading hierarchy;
- semantic landmarks;
- labelled form controls;
- programmatically associated validation errors;
- adequate colour contrast;
- reduced-motion support;
- no content requiring hover only;
- meaningful link text;
- accessible menus and dialogs;
- appropriate alternative text;
- zoom and reflow support;
- no horizontal scrolling at standard mobile widths except intentionally scrollable data regions.

Automated testing is necessary but does not replace manual keyboard and screen-reader-oriented inspection.

## 27. Responsive requirements

The site must be designed and visually verified at minimum for:

- 390 × 844 mobile;
- 768 × 1024 tablet portrait;
- 1024 × 1366 tablet or compact desktop;
- 1440 × 900 desktop;
- 1672 × 941 reference desktop;
- 1920 × 1080 large desktop.

Layouts must adapt structurally, not merely shrink. Navigation, hero composition, card grids, diagrams, forms and tables must remain legible and operable.

## 28. Performance requirements

Performance must be tested on production-equivalent assets and hosting.

Targets for representative public pages:

- Largest Contentful Paint: at or below 2.5 seconds at the 75th percentile where operationally measurable;
- Interaction to Next Paint: at or below 200 milliseconds at the 75th percentile;
- Cumulative Layout Shift: at or below 0.1;
- no unnecessarily blocking third-party scripts;
- responsive images with appropriate formats and dimensions;
- font loading that avoids invisible text and severe layout shift;
- compressed, cached static assets;
- JavaScript limited to actual interaction requirements.

Laboratory results must be interpreted alongside real hosting and network conditions.

## 29. SEO requirements

Every indexable page must include:

- unique title;
- unique meta description;
- one clear primary heading;
- logical heading hierarchy;
- canonical URL;
- useful internal links;
- descriptive image alternative text where appropriate;
- social-sharing metadata;
- inclusion in `sitemap.xml`;
- appropriate structured data based only on verified facts.

The site must avoid thin, repetitive service pages. Each page must answer a distinct user or procurement question.

## 30. Security and privacy requirements

- HTTPS must be enforced;
- no credentials or tokens in client assets or repository history;
- forms must validate and sanitise input server-side;
- file uploads, if enabled, require explicit type, size, storage and malware-handling controls;
- dependencies must be maintained and reviewed;
- security headers must be configured appropriately;
- privacy and cookie behaviour must match actual processing;
- personal information must be minimised and retained only under an approved operational policy;
- analytics and marketing tools must not be added without documented consent and privacy implications.

## 31. Analytics and operational measurement

Before launch, Techgrity must decide whether analytics are necessary and identify the approved tool.

Useful events may include:

- project CTA selections;
- completed project enquiries;
- capability statement downloads;
- document requests;
- contact link selections;
- navigation to key capability and industry pages.

Analytics must not create false conversion claims and must remain subordinate to privacy requirements.

## 32. Browser support

The website must support current stable versions of:

- Chrome;
- Edge;
- Firefox;
- Safari;
- mobile Safari;
- Chrome on Android.

Graceful degradation is required where an advanced visual effect is unavailable.

## 33. Content management and implementation architecture

The implementation approach must be selected after content and master-template approval. Regardless of framework or CMS choice, it must provide:

- reusable global components;
- reusable page-family templates;
- central navigation and footer data;
- structured metadata;
- maintainable image handling;
- clear separation of content, presentation and environment secrets;
- testable form integrations;
- reproducible builds;
- source control through this repository;
- production deployment traceability.

The project must not prematurely adopt a complex CMS or application framework without a maintenance need that justifies it.

## 36. Delivery stages

### Stage 1 — Architecture

- lock sitemap, navigation, URLs and templates;
- complete.

### Stage 2 — Content specification

- define exact page messages, sections, headings, calls to action, links and factual dependencies;
- current next stage.

### Stage 3 — Visual system and art direction

- define reusable design tokens and components;
- define image requirements and responsive crops.

### Stage 4 — Authoritative master visuals

- create and approve the remaining master page designs;
- record approval evidence.

### Stage 5 — Implementation

- implement shared components and all page families;
- integrate real documents and forms.

### Stage 6 — Evidence-backed QA

- validate content, responsive layouts, browsers, accessibility, SEO, performance, links, forms and downloads;
- visually compare implemented pages to approved references.

### Stage 7 — Deployment and commissioning

- deploy to the approved environment;
- verify DNS, TLS, redirects, form routing, monitoring and public behaviour;
- publish only after acceptance criteria pass.

## 37. Acceptance criteria

The website may be declared ready only when all of the following are directly proven:

- all approved public and system routes exist;
- primary navigation and mega-menus are correct and accessible;
- every page uses approved final copy;
- all factual claims are verified;
- all displayed documents download successfully;
- all forms validate, submit, route and confirm correctly;
- responsive layouts are visually verified at required viewports;
- no horizontal overflow or broken content exists;
- automated and manual accessibility checks are complete;
- page titles, descriptions, canonicals, structured data, sitemap and robots behaviour are correct;
- broken-link and asset checks pass;
- performance is tested on production-equivalent delivery;
- privacy, cookie and analytics behaviour matches the published policies;
- implementation visually matches the approved master-page references;
- deployment, DNS and TLS are confirmed from the public internet;
- no unsupported completion, client, certification or partner claims remain.
